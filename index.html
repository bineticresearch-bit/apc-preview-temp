A whole-home clean, not a vent wipe-down

The dust you see is only part of the story.

Most of the material in a forced-air system sits out of sight — on the walls of supply and return runs, in the plenums, and around the blower. When the system starts, some of it moves.

We put the system under negative air pressure with a HEPA vacuum, seal every register, and clean one run at a time. Starting at the vent farthest from the furnace, we agitate the duct with forced-air rods and a 140 psi tool while the vacuum pulls the loosened debris out of the system.

When we are finished, we open the vacuum and show you what came out. No mystery, no pressure, no surprise add-ons at the door.

Good questions

Air duct cleaning questions, answered

How much does air duct cleaning cost?

Pricing is based on the square footage of the home and the number of vents. A short phone conversation is usually enough to give you an exact price. The price quoted on the phone is the price you pay when we finish — there are no hidden fees and no surprise upselling at the door.

What are the $49 and $99 duct cleaning specials?

Offers that low are usually an introduction, not a complete whole-home cleaning. The low price may cover one vent, one system, or a limited service, and the final bill can climb once the crew is inside. Ask exactly what is included, how many vents are covered, and whether the price changes after an inspection. A legitimate quote should be clear before work begins.

How often should air ducts be cleaned?

NADCA recommends cleaning every three to five years for many homes. Homes with pets, allergies, recent renovations, or heavy dust may need service sooner. If you have never had the ducts cleaned in a home you just bought, an inspection is a practical first step.

How long does a thorough air duct cleaning take?

Most whole-home cleanings take three to four hours. The exact time depends on the number of vents, the size and layout of the system, and how much debris is present. A company that promises a complete cleaning in a couple of hours may be leaving material behind.

How do you clean the air ducts?

We connect a negative-air HEPA vacuum to the supply plenum, seal every register, and clean one run at a time. Starting at the vent farthest from the furnace, we feed forced-air rods and a 140 psi agitation tool through the duct. The vacuum pulls the loosened debris out of the system instead of pushing it into the room. We repeat the process on the return side.

Will duct cleaning leave dust in my house?

No. The system stays under negative air pressure while each vent is cleaned, so loosened debris travels into the HEPA vacuum. We also protect floors and furnishings and clean up the work area before we leave.
