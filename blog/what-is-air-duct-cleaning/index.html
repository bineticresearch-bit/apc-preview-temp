What Is Air Duct Cleaning? A Homeowner's Guide

Air duct cleaning is more than vacuuming the registers. Here is what a thorough whole-home cleaning involves and what it can — and cannot — do for your home.

October 6, 2026Duct cleaning basicsBy All Pro Duct Cleaning

Air duct cleaning is the process of removing dust, debris, and other material that has settled inside a home's heating and cooling ductwork. It is not the same as changing a furnace filter or vacuuming a register. A thorough cleaning reaches the inside surfaces of the supply and return runs, the plenums, and the accessible components around the furnace.

The goal is simple: remove the material that has accumulated so the system can move air without carrying that buildup into the rooms. It does not sterilize the home, cure allergies, or replace HVAC maintenance. It is one part of keeping a forced-air system clean.

What happens during a thorough cleaning

A proper whole-home cleaning starts with an inspection. The technician counts the vents, locates the furnace, and identifies any fragile or inaccessible sections. Then the system is placed under negative air pressure with a HEPA vacuum connected to the supply plenum. Every register is sealed so the vacuum has one path to pull from.

Working from the vent farthest from the furnace toward the furnace, the technician feeds air-driven rods and an agitation tool through each run. The agitation loosens material from the duct walls; the vacuum carries it out of the system. The returns are cleaned the same way after the supply side is finished.

Accessible furnace components — the blower area, A/C coils, and plenums — are cleaned as part of the service. The system is reassembled, filters are replaced, and the furnace is run briefly to confirm normal operation.

What gets removed

What comes out of a duct system depends on the home. Common material includes:

  • Household dust, lint, and pet hair
  • Drywall dust, sawdust, and construction debris after a remodel
  • Pollen and outdoor particulate that entered through the return
  • Small toys, food wrappers, and other objects dropped into registers
  • Rodent nesting material or droppings in severe cases
  • Loose debris that settled in the plenums and main trunks

In a home that has never had the ducts cleaned, it is not unusual to find several pounds of material. In a newer home that was cleaned recently, there may be very little. That is why an inspection matters more than a sales script.

What duct cleaning will not fix

Duct cleaning is not a cure-all. It will not correct a furnace that is failing, a filter that is too restrictive, a duct system that is disconnected, or a crawl space that is pumping moisture into the home. It also will not guarantee lower energy bills. Those problems need their own solution.

If a company promises that cleaning will eliminate allergies, instantly cut your utility bill, or solve every air quality complaint, ask for the details behind the claim. A good contractor will explain what cleaning can reasonably do and what else may be contributing to the problem.

Signs your ducts may need attention

  • Dust puffs from a register when the system starts.
  • The home smells musty or stale when the heat or A/C runs.
  • You have pets, allergies, or a recent remodel.
  • The ducts have not been cleaned in three to five years.
  • Debris or pet hair is visible inside the first few feet of a vent.
  • One room has noticeably weaker airflow than the rest.

What we see in Vancouver and Portland

In Clark County and the Portland metro, we commonly find a mix of older rigid ductwork, flex runs added during remodels, and long dryer vent lines tucked into walls. Wet winters also make crawl space moisture a factor. If the crawl space is damp or the vapor barrier is torn, cleaning the ducts without addressing that moisture is a short-term fix.
