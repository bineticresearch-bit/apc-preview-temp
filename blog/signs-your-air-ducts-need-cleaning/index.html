8 Signs Your Air Ducts May Need Cleaning

Not every dusty home needs duct cleaning. These eight signs are the ones worth paying attention to before you book an appointment.

October 14, 2026Duct cleaning basicsBy All Pro Duct Cleaning

Dust is a normal part of living in a home. So is a little pet hair, a little pollen, and the occasional cobweb. The question is not whether your home has dust — it is whether the duct system is adding to it. These eight signs are a practical starting point.

The eight signs

1. Dust puffs from a register

If you see a puff of dust when the furnace or air conditioner starts, the system is moving material that had settled in the duct or near the register. It may be a small amount, or it may be a sign of a larger buildup.

2. The home smells musty when the system runs

A musty smell that appears only when the heat or A/C is on often points to moisture somewhere in the system or the crawl space. Cleaning may help, but the moisture source needs to be found.

3. You have pets

Pet hair and dander collect in return ducts and on blower components. Homes with multiple pets or long-haired breeds tend to need attention sooner than the three-to-five-year guideline.

4. You recently remodeled

Drywall dust, sawdust, and small construction debris can settle into nearby returns and supply runs. If the system was running during the work, some of that material has already circulated.

5. Airflow is weak in one or more rooms

A weak register can be caused by a closed damper, a crushed flex run, a disconnected joint, or a dirty system. Cleaning alone will not fix a disconnected duct, so an inspection should come first.

6. The ducts have not been cleaned in years

NADCA suggests cleaning every three to five years for many homes. If you have owned the home for years and never had the ducts cleaned, it is reasonable to have them inspected.

7. You see debris inside the vent

Shine a light into the first few feet of a supply or return. If you see heavy dust, pet hair, or objects, the rest of the run may also have buildup.

8. Allergy symptoms seem worse indoors

Duct cleaning is not a medical treatment, and it will not eliminate allergies. But if dust and irritants are accumulating in the system, reducing that reservoir may be one useful step alongside filtration, humidity control, and source control.

What to check first

Before you book a cleaning, check the filter. A clogged filter restricts airflow and can make the system feel dirtier than it is. Confirm the filter size and change it according to the manufacturer's recommendation. Look at the return grille and the first few feet of the duct. If the furnace or A/C has not been serviced recently, have an HVAC technician inspect the equipment separately.

If the crawl space is damp, address that too. A musty odor can travel from the crawl space into the living area even when the ducts are clean.

What happens next

A good duct cleaning company will inspect the system before quoting. They should be able to explain how many vents are included, how the system will be put under negative pressure, and whether the furnace and returns are part of the scope. If the answer is vague, ask again.
